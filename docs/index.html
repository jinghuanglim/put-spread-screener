What the screen does

The three gates, and what they do not cover

Each name is tested in order. Any failure drops it and nothing downstream can rescue it — these are vetoes, not scores, so there is no "good enough on balance".

Gate 1 · trend
Last price must be above the average of the previous 20 trading days. Below it, the name is out, however attractive the premium. The average uses completed days only, so today’s part-formed bar cannot flatter it.
Gate 2 · earnings
No scheduled results on or before expiry. An earnings date inside the window is the single largest source of the overnight gap this structure cannot survive. Dates come from two independent sources; when they disagree the name is flagged rather than guessed at. Index funds have no earnings, so they are tested against CPI, jobs and Fed days instead.
Gate 3 · news
Not automated. Headlines are printed below for a person to read. A guidance cut, a probe, a downgrade cluster or sector contagion is a veto no matter how rich the premium looks. Nothing here decides that for you.
width filter
The two strikes must sit at least $5 apart, which rules out anything trading under roughly $100.

What it does not do: it does not price anything, check that the credit is worth the risk, size a position, or know what you already hold. A name appearing here means it survived three filters — nothing more. Most days the honest answer is to do nothing.
